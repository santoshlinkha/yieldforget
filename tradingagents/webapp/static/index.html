      Stocks: quality checklist, Wall Street, signals and every model's ranking

      For each S&P 500 stock (and your watchlist): the ticker page's quality checklist (moat, financial strength, profitability) as one score, valuation kept apart, and Wall Street's consensus and price-target upside, combined into one rank. Signals and action shows the stock models' trend, breakout and crash warnings and the daily decision's ENTER / WAIT / HOLD / EXIT. Click a column to sort; click a ticker for its full page.

      What the columns mean
      How the scores and ranks are measured
      • Quality score (0–100): the share of the ticker page's tests the stock passes: the moat checks (gross margin, return on capital, margin stability, free cash flow, and so on) plus the financial strength items (debt to equity, interest coverage, current ratio) and the profitability items (FCF, ROIC above 15%, margin trends). A pass counts 1, a "check" ½, a flag 0; tests without data are left out. Example: 13 of 14 tests passed = 92.9.
      • Quality rank: position by quality score alone, 1 = highest. Equal scores share a rank (many stocks score 100).
      • Consensus: this month's analyst ratings averaged on 1 = strong buy, 2 = buy, 3 = hold, 4 = sell, 5 = strong sell. Lower is more bullish.
      • Target upside: the mean analyst price target ÷ the last close − 1.
      • Combined score (0–100): each stock gets three percentiles among the stocks screened (quality score, consensus with stronger buys higher, target upside); the combined score is their average × 100. 100 would be the best on all three. Hover over a score to see its three parts. A stock missing one measure is averaged on the other two (noted next to its name).
      • Combined rank: stocks ordered by combined score, 1 = highest; ties go to the higher quality score, then the ticker.
      • Signals: the stock models' probabilities over 20 sessions, each compared with how often it happened to any stock in training (the lift: 1.0× is ordinary) and with today's other stocks (a warning also needs the top fifth, so a market-wide shift does not flag everyone). Breakout likely: P(the price trades above its 20-day high at any time in the next 20 sessions, intraday) ≥ 1.2× usual. Breakdown risk: P(it trades below its 20-day low, intraday) ≥ 1.2× usual: a barrier touch, not a loss, a crash or a limit fill. Crash warning: P(a large loss at the 20-session end) ≥ 1.5× usual and ≥ 10%. Deep drawdown warning: ≥ 1.5× usual and ≥ 20%. A model that gives nearly every stock the same probability raises no warning and says so. Trend: up, down or sideways when its probability is ≥ 1.15× its usual share and in today's top fifth; otherwise "no clear trend". Action: the latest daily decision (Decisions tab); only its top candidates and Trading holdings get one. None of it is validated for trading.
      • Valuation is shown but not in the score, so a good company and a cheap one stay distinguishable. None of these was tested for predicting returns; it is a screen of facts and opinions as of today.

      Every stock model, fitted on all history to the last close and refitted every 20 sessions, ranks today's S&P 500 and your watchlist (100 = its top pick). Tested IC: rank correlation with the net trade result on years the model had not seen (2012–24 · 2025 on); about 0 means no skill. Only the deciding ranking decides anything, and none is validated.

      The model's picks

      Cumulative value of $1 on each recommendation, per $1 put in

      Each group's worth divided by what it put in, so groups of different sizes share one axis: $1.00 is break-even. Held positions at each day's close less the entry half of the cost; sold ones at their 20th close less the whole cost.

      The second model's suggestions

      The policy layer (buy at the next close, buy on a limit, or cash). It abstains ("insufficient evidence") until its validation passes, so these are the buys it would have made, recorded and judged anyway. A limit that never filled is not judged.

      The AI agents' calls

      Forecast models

      Return forecasts made on this machine from Yahoo prices: no paid AI. A forecast is a log return above cash over its horizon in trading sessions, not a yearly rate, with a 5-95% range and the chance of a gain. These are model estimates, not outcomes; the evidence for each model is its walk-forward record below.

      Models

      Side by side: the runs just made

      Evidence columns come from each model's walk-forward trial at that horizon. Agrees: mean rank correlation of its forecasts with the other models' at the same horizon (1 = same order). Click a row to see its forecasts below.

      Live record: forecasts scored after the fact

      Every saved ETF run, scored once its horizon has passed, on prices no model can have seen in training. This is the clean test for Chronos-2, which may have seen past prices in pretraining: the app records its forecasts at each new close when it starts. A handful of dates says little; the record means something after a few dozen.

      Model comparison (walk-forward, out of sample)

      Each row was refitted every year on data available then, and scored on the years after. IC: how well the forecast ranked assets on each date (0 is chance). R²: whether its sizes beat "no return above cash" (negative is worse). Calibration: 1 if sizes are right, near 0 if far too confident. Coverage: share of outcomes inside the 5-95% range (target 90%). Net top fifth: per period, after 10 bp a side. p (Holm): chance the IC is luck, after counting every model tried. Feature importance is not causation.

      Settings

      The money settings, the agents' models, and the ETFs to forecast (holdings and trades are on the Portfolio tab). Kept in portfolio.sqlite3; the ETF list in its file next to the app.

      Money

      The money set aside for buying stocks. The model sizes buys from what is left of it: the budget less what goes into stocks, plus what comes back from sales. Saving a new amount starts the count again from today. It moves no money.

      Companies the model scores

      Every company in the S&P 500 is scored after each close. Add any other US-listed stocks here (tickers, separated by commas or spaces): they are scored the same way, ranked against the S&P 500, and can be picked as the best buy. Funds are not scored by the stock model.

      Every morning

      While the app is running: after the time below, once for each new close, it records the fund forecasts, scores every stock with every model and makes the daily decision. Models are refitted only when their last fit is the number of trading sessions below old (and the ensemble's weights then too); the same inputs are never run twice.

      Each morning, make and save
      Each is saved. On the same data and models it is loaded, not made again; it is made anew only after a new close, a refit or a code change.

      API keys

      Stored in the app's .env file on this computer (never committed) and used at once, without a restart. The page only ever shows a masked form. Leave a box empty and press Remove to delete a key.

      Agent models

      Which provider and model each agent runs on. Putting the two sides of each debate on different providers (bull vs bear, aggressive vs conservative) stops one model arguing with itself; a third provider can judge. Blank rows use the default. Every model needs a price (dollars per million tokens) so its cost stays within the AI budget; a model with no price is refused before any call is made.
