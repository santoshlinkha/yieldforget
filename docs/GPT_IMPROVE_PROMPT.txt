You are a senior quantitative portfolio researcher reviewing my personal portfolio optimizer.
I want it to be state of the art for a small, long-only, retail Roth IRA. Read the description,
then give me a prioritized improvement plan. Be specific and honest: tell me where a fancy method
would not help an account this size, and where my current method is weak.

## The account
- Robinhood Roth IRA, cash account, about $5,750. Selling is not taxed. No commissions.
- Long only, fractional shares, no margin, no shorting, no options trading.
- Settlement is T+1: buys may only use settled cash.
- SGOV (0-3 month T-bills) is cash waiting to be invested.
- About $27.61 of new money is invested every trading day.

## What the program does today (Python; numpy, pandas, yfinance; no scipy)

Data
- Adjusted daily closes from Yahoo Finance over each ticker's full history, cached per day.
- Universe: my holdings plus groups I switch on or off: about 40 long-lived ETFs (one per market
  corner, bonds and gold included), today's S&P 500 members, and "disruptive theme" stocks and
  ETFs (quantum, AI, nuclear, robotics, space, crypto, EVs, drones, genomics). About 566 funds are
  scanned. A scanned fund needs at least 3 years of prices.

Risk
- Stress loss per asset = its deepest peak-to-trough drawdown in its whole history. A young fund
  borrows the drawdown of an older fund on the same index (QQQM from QQQ, AVUV from VBR); if it
  has under 10 years of history and no proxy, its drawdown is floored at 50%.
- Portfolio stress loss = sum of (weight x stress loss), i.e. every holding hits its worst drop
  at the same time. This is a HARD constraint.
- Risk dial 1-10 sets that limit: 1%, 2.5%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 30%, 40%, 50%, none.
- Per-asset caps: at most 35% of the portfolio, and at most 25% of the loss budget.
- Covariance: sample covariance of daily returns over 10 years (pairwise, then clipped to PSD).

Expected returns
- Expected excess return = volatility x Sharpe, where the Sharpe is shrunk toward a common
  prior of 0.3: trust = 0.5 x min(years of history / 10, 1);
  Sharpe = trust x clip(historical Sharpe, -1, 1.5) + (1 - trust) x 0.3.
  The historical Sharpe is measured against the cash return on the same days.
- Ranked by compound growth: expected excess minus 0.5 x variance.
- Optional AI tilt: a multi-agent LLM system (market-technicals analyst and news analyst;
  a fundamentals analyst exists but is off for this optimizer) rates each holding Buy /
  Overweight / Hold / Underweight / Sell. Each notch above Hold adds 0.15 to that asset's Sharpe.

Optimization
- Mean-variance: maximize excess.x - (3/2) x'Sx, subject to 0 <= x <= caps, sum(x) <= 1, and
  stress loss <= limit. Solved with an augmented Lagrangian and accelerated projected gradient.
  Whatever is not invested sits in SGOV.
- Trades under 0.5% of the portfolio are skipped.

Output and tracking
- Daily orders: sells today, buys the next trading day once the sales settle (T+1), and how to
  spread the daily recurring buys across funds under target.
- A table of the trade-off at every risk level (invested %, stress loss, expected growth).
- A paper tracker since 2026-10-22: "followed the plan" vs "kept as is", filled at the close.
- A walk-forward backtest that uses only the prices available at each rebalance date. It cannot
  test the AI ratings, ignores survivorship bias (today's scan list) and assumes free rebalancing.
- A local web app, plus a daily market brief: index, sector and VIX returns explained by an LLM
  from cited headlines and FRED macro data.

What it does NOT have
- No growth-rate target mode (I can only pick a loss limit, not "aim for X% a year").
- No regime detection (HMM, Markov switching, change-point detection).
- No volatility forecasting (GARCH, EWMA, realized vol) and no option-implied volatility or skew.
- No covariance shrinkage, factor model, CVaR or scenario-based risk.
- No analyst consensus estimates, earnings revisions or price targets. Point-in-time SEC EDGAR
  statements exist in the codebase but feed only the LLM fundamentals analyst, which is off here.
- No transaction-cost or turnover model beyond the 0.5% band.
